## What's genuinely new about NestQuest

No single product we found joins design generation, cost and feasibility checking for one user. Design generators (Maket, Planner 5D, RoomSketcher) make plans and visuals but do not check whether the house fits the user's budget or lot. Cost estimators (StartBuild, Architectural Designs' report) price a plan you already have, but do not design one. Developer feasibility tools (TestFit, Deepblocks, Architechtures) do unit mix, massing and financials, but cost $50 to $1,250+ per month, are built for professionals, and do not handle single-family homes. NestQuest's new contribution is the **combination**:

- **Budget-first, multi-agent generation.** You give a budget, lot and preferences, and you get three buildable options.
- **Deterministic, cited cost math.** Every dollar figure comes from a calculator using NAHB cost per sq ft and state cost factors, not from the language model, so the numbers are consistent and traceable.
- **Automatic pass/fail screening of each option.** Each option is checked against budget, setbacks, lot coverage, height and room-size minimums.
- **One intake for two kinds of users.** The same flow serves a homeowner (single-family) and a small developer (duplex, fourplex, townhomes or garden apartments with unit mix, parking, density, total development cost and yield on cost).
- **Chat refinement that keeps those guarantees, plus 2D plans and a furnished 3D model.**

Compared with a general chatbot, the novelty is the grounding: the LLM proposes designs, but the numbers and the compliance checks come from code.

## Where NestQuest is weaker

NestQuest is a student prototype and an early-stage screening tool, not a replacement for professional tools.

- **Cost data is coarse.** A national NAHB average times a state factor is less precise than StartBuild's ZIP-level, 60-line-item data or Zonda Envision's live builder option pricing.
- **Zoning checks are generic.** They use typical setback and coverage rules, not real parcel ordinances like Deepblocks, and they are not permit-grade like Archistar's PreCheck.
- **Plans are concept-level.** Maket and RoomSketcher give more detailed, editable floor plans, and Architechtures exports real BIM/CAD. Houseplans.com and Architectural Designs sell construction-ready drawings by professional designers, which NestQuest does not.
- **Multifamily analysis is simplified.** TestFit generates real site plans with parking geometry, roads, topography and a linked pro forma, so a serious developer would still need it, or a civil engineer and architect.
- **It is unproven.** NestQuest has no track record of homes actually built from its output, and its cost estimates have not been checked against real Engee Homes bids.

Its honest value is as a fast, free, grounded first pass that tells a buyer or small developer "is this feasible, roughly what will it cost, and what could it look like" before paying for those professional tools.
